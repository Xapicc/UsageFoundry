# The dashboard's "waiting on you" line

[← testing index](../testing.md)

Read before adding or editing tests of `waitingView.ts`, of `GET /api/waiting` or of `landableBranches`.

`waitingView.test.ts` and `src/app/api/waiting/route.test.ts` are the dashboard's "waiting on you" line and the five counts behind it, and they fail the same way in both directions: a number that says less than is known, or more. A read that failed and printed as 0 tells the operator nothing is waiting when nothing was asked; a count taken over one page of branches and printed as the whole hides the older unmerged branch it missed; and the quiet sentence over either is the same claim in words. Nothing throws and the page looks calm.

`waitingView.test.ts` pins the pure half. A known zero is left out and the rest keep their order and their links, and the three counts a page can narrow — needs review, failed, operator-only tasks — link to the page already narrowed to the rows they were taken over (`/runs?status=needs-review`, `/runs?status=failed`, `/tasks?lane=operator`), because a spelling the page does not know is refused there by name and a bare `/runs` lands on every run. Every count read in full and zero is the one quiet line. A failed read is named ("Branches ready to land: unknown") and keeps the line from going quiet, and known and unknown counts mix. A floor is said as "At least N", and a floor of zero is "not all checked" rather than dropped. One is singular, and a failed poll wins over an answer held from before it.

The route test is over a throwaway database and a real git repository, because "ready to land" is git's answer about a branch and not a column. Needs-review counts at any age; failed counts only inside the last 24 hours, and not a stopped run; proposals count `pending` in any chat and not `approved`, `rejected` or `superseded`; tasks count `open` and operator-only, and not a claimed, closed or agent one; branches count the one branch a commit ahead of its target and neither the merged one nor the one whose ref is gone, which is `isQueueableBranch` — the rule the branches page draws its tick boxes by. A last case drops a table and asserts that one count comes back `unknown` with a reason while the other four still arrive. The failed runs are not placed on the 24-hour boundary itself, because the route reads its own clock a few milliseconds after the fixture does.

Mutations in the compiled emit, measured 2026-11-08: dropping `status: "open"` from the board's read fails two route cases, dropping the 24-hour window fails one, dropping `b.exists` from `isQueueableBranch` fails two, rendering an unknown as nothing fails two `waitingView` cases, and letting a floor of zero vanish fails one.
