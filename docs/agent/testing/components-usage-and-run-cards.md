# Components: usage, context and run cards

[← testing index](../testing.md)

Read before adding or editing tests of `UsagePeriods`, `ContextOccupancy`, `ContextControl`, `RunPruning`, `InstallSpendCard`, `BranchWork`, `RunHandoff`, `RecentBlocksCard`, `OpenTasksChart` and `windowCardNotes`.

The eight renderings besides `Meter.test.tsx`, `LiveTelemetry.test.tsx`, `Markdown.test.tsx` and `ui/Table.test.tsx` are `UsagePeriods.test.tsx`, `ContextOccupancy.test.tsx`, `ContextControl.test.tsx`, `RunPruning.test.tsx`, `InstallSpendCard.test.tsx`, `ui/Disclosure.test.tsx`, `ui/LimitField.test.tsx` and `ui/ListView.test.tsx`, and they are on those four's argument again.

`UsagePeriods` prints a percentage against a ceiling Anthropic does not publish: a day and a month are measured against the weekly one spread over their own length, which is a **pace** — 254% of a pro-rated $314 on the transcripts it was written against — so what is pinned is that the sentence saying where the number came from cannot be wired apart from the number, and it fails in both directions, since the same sentence over a weekly view implies that window is unguarded.

`ContextOccupancy` prints a percentage against a ceiling that has already moved twice — 167,000, 300,000, 200,000 — which is the whole reason `ceilingTokens` travels on the DTO, so the pair that earns it is the reading at the ceiling it was handed *and* the same series against a different one: a component dividing by a literal would satisfy the first alone and go on drawing the old figure after the next move, silently. Its freshness cases are the second thing it earns, and they came from an operator report rather than from an argument: a run inside one sub-agent gains no sample for as long as that lasts — 22 minutes, measured — and the panel, which had only the newest point's timestamp, said "read 22m ago" over a figure that was correct the whole time. So the age is asserted to come off `lastCheck` and *not* off the point, with the hold named separately; a fixture whose two timestamps differ by a rounding would pass either way, which is why the read is twenty seconds old and the point twenty-two minutes older than the read. Three more pin the ways the same line goes wrong in the other direction — a gap inside one read interval named at all, which is every tool call and trains an operator to stop reading the line; a missing `lastCheck` borrowing the point's age rather than admitting a restart has read nothing; and an `unreadable` read rendered as freshness, which is the one wording that leaves the panel worse than absent. The composition stack's own case reads a band's top and bottom edge off the path it was drawn as, at the rightmost x, and asserts the one is above the other for every provenance with tokens — because the stack sized its axis to the window, clamped every running total at it, and so drew every band above the crossing with its top on its bottom whenever the residual was negative, which on this install was every reading of a run once it carried tool traffic. It fails on the old chart at `conversation` and passes on the new one, and it pins the rest of the fix beside that: the hatched strip and the residual's hatched swatch present exactly when the bands claim more than the window, the signed figure in the legend, and the sentence in the description, with a reading that claims no more than its window drawing none of it.

Its other three are on `UsagePeriods`' argument reached from the run page.

A run with no readings must draw `Meter`'s hatch rather than an empty fill, because "nothing measured" and "measured at nothing" are opposite statements and the same bar — the invariant `Meter.test.tsx` already pins, here at the one caller whose unmeasured state is the *ordinary* first second of a run.

A prune mark is pinned as **geometry** rather than as existence, since a marker drawn at a fixed offset, or against the wrong end of the span, still renders a marker: an unexplained cliff in the line is what an operator reads as a bug in the graph, and a mark in the wrong place is worse than none because it asserts a time the cut did not happen — which is also why a prune older than the oldest returned sample is counted in the caption rather than clamped to the edge.

The caption's currency sentence was pinned here on `LiveTelemetry.test.tsx`'s argument, and that assertion is gone with the sentence: the operator read the standing paragraph under the chart as wall-of-text and asked for it, so there is no on-screen copy left to pin. The hazard is unchanged — this page carries a prune's `tokensRemoved` in `contextTokens` and this series in `apiContextTokens`, which differ by tens of thousands **in both directions**, so subtracting one from the other typechecks and produces an entirely ordinary number — and it is on record now in `ContextOccupancy.tsx`'s module doc and in the sr-only prune table's warning at its own figures, neither of which a test can hold to the screen. What the suite pins in its place is the failure the removal introduced: with every clause left in that paragraph conditional, an ordinary series must render **no caption element at all** rather than a `<p>` that is empty and still takes its bottom margin, and whichever clause does render must open the paragraph without the leading separator each of them carried while prose ran before it.

The fallback-basis cases carry their own control for `LiveTelemetry`'s reason — a component that printed "this is a byte estimate" unconditionally would satisfy both directions and mean nothing.

`ContextControl` and `RunPruning` print the same `PruneSavingsDTO.netUSD` that `PruneSavingsRows` prints in the band lower down, and that DTO carries two fields whose only job is to say what the figure is not: an unsettled prune is already in the saving and not yet in what buying it cost, so the net can only come down, and an unpriced one is in neither half, so the money covers fewer prunes than the count printed beside it. Both rendered neither, which is the one direction this app's own copy says it must not mislead in — an upper bound wearing a net's clothes, on the two surfaces an operator opens first. Each pins a fault *and* its control, because a card that hedges unconditionally says nothing and would satisfy the positive cases alone; and the two are pinned apart rather than together, since marking an unpriced prune "at most" claims a sign the reading does not carry. `RunPruning` keeps both halves. **`ContextControl` keeps only the ceiling half**, because the tile no longer prints a coverage line or a footnote about the overlap: both were removed by the operator, restored by an agent reading the invariant that then licensed them, and removed again along with it. What stands in their place is one test asserting that neither string renders. It pins a rendering rather than a behaviour, which everything else in this file calls a liability, and it is here for the one reason that outranks that: this copy has been re-added once already by a pass that found the page and the docs disagreeing, and a test is the only part of the disagreement a build run cannot read past.

`InstallSpendCard` draws one bar from two figures — `spentUSD` measured and `spentGuardUSD` counting the cycles in flight — and the card printed the first as the bar and the second as the line beneath it, so its own head and its own detail were different readings with nothing naming either: "50.0% – 80.0%" over "$8.00 of $10.00", which divides out to the upper band. What is pinned is that the detail names the measured figure *and* the guard's, since dropping the second leaves `Meter`'s hatched band out to 80% unexplained, which is the fault `Meter.test.tsx` pins from the other end. The first half of that is pinned as the *pair* and not as either string — the amount the line leads with, over the ceiling it names, must be the fraction the bar was given — because a test on the string alone passes again the next time the two are wired apart. The guard's figure is named only while it is higher, and that bound is pinned from the other side on `Meter`'s own rule: an equal upper reading draws no band, so on an idle install a second amount would be money with nothing on the bar to account for it, reading as two measurements that happen to agree rather than as one. The no-limit branch is pinned separately because it is the one this app ships in and it had the over-count's caveat left behind in the branch an operator has to configure their way into: a test on the configured branch alone would have passed throughout.

`BranchWork.test.tsx` is the thirteenth rendering and is on the same argument reached from the branches table: `uncommitted` is three-valued and the row read it with `!!`, so a checkout nothing probed — the deterministic fate of every row past `MAX_PENDING_PROBES`, in page order — drew exactly what a checkout git called clean draws. Nothing throws, nothing fails to typecheck, and the page a reader acts on says a run that could not commit wrote nothing. The gate on Commit is pinned beside it because it read the same `!!` and fails the other way round: it withheld the one door that saves the work from the row that most needed it, while Purge and Delete stayed on it. Both are invisible from every file except the rendered row, which is what puts them in this class rather than under a function's test.

`RunHandoff.test.tsx` is the sixteenth rendering, and it is on this list because `orchestrator` writes `payload.merge` only when the operator's checkout is clean — when it is dirty it writes `payload.mergeBlocked` instead and no merge line at all, since a copyable command gets copied and this one would merge on top of uncommitted work. Nothing enforces that split but the ternary in this component: `payload` is `Record<string, unknown>`, so a branch reaching for the wrong key renders `undefined` rather than failing a typecheck, and a branch rendering both would look right in every screenshot. The rest of its cases pin the three-way split the payload's untyped shape invites — a missing array, an empty one and a populated one are three different renders, and `Array.isArray` is the only thing telling them apart.

`RecentBlocksCard.test.tsx` is the seventeenth rendering, and its Tokens column is a sum the card does itself, which is the whole risk: `TokenCountsDTO` has six members and a window's volume is all six, and dropping one — `cacheWriteUnattributed` is the one that arrived last and is easiest to forget, and it is 100% of the write volume on a transcript written before the TTL split shipped — understates every row against the meter this table exists to break down, while the column still holds a plausible number, the typecheck stays green, and no total on the page is drawn across these rows to disagree with. Its tail is pinned on `docs/agent/git-and-review.md`'s reason about a shortened diff: a list that stops at fifteen saying nothing reads as the whole set, and the count is drawn from the same constant that does the slicing, so the two cannot be wired apart.

`OpenTasksChart.test.tsx` is the eighteenth rendering, and most of it is the pure `openTaskSeries` beside the component rather than the picture, because every way that function goes wrong draws an ordinary line: days cut in UTC move each change on the taskboard's week two hours early in Berlin, days measured as `now - n * 86_400_000` put every sample an hour off midnight in the week a clock change falls in, and an inequality turned the wrong way counts a task closed at a sample instant as still open there. The file pins `TZ=Europe/Berlin` for its own process and asserts the samples as UTC instants, so a wrong cut is a different number rather than a constructor that moves with it. The rendering cases pin the `aria-label` carrying every count, the count and signed change printed beside the line, the reconstruction caveat, and that a flat series — zero included — draws a path and not `NaN`, which is what a plot scaled by its own zero range becomes. `closedTaskSeries` is held to the same zone and the same instants, and the cases that earn it are the ones that move one close between two plausible days: a close at a day's last millisecond counted on both days that instant borders or on neither, the day before the week counted into it, and a close cut in UTC. One case asserts that the week's closes equal what was filed less the open line's change, which is what keeps the two charts telling one story; the rendering cases pin the per-day `aria-label`, today's count and the week's total, the done-and-dropped caveat, and a closed week of nothing drawn on the floor rather than `NaN`.

`windowCardNotes.test.ts` is not a rendering. It pins the sentences under the dashboard's two meters, and it builds a real `buildSnapshot` for each case, because the defect was the card and the snapshot disagreeing about which provider readings were still in force. The card read the raw `plan`, which `planUsage` re-serves for up to an hour, after `buildSnapshot` had dropped a rolled-over wall and 5-hour instant. It printed a closed week's Opus 95% and called a derived reset "reported by Anthropic", and both sentences look right. So the rolled-over case asserts no wall line and the derived provenance, including under a future pinned reset, which the stale provider instant outranks in `buildSnapshot`. The wall-only case asserts no "all-model" claim over a bar that is the Opus wall. The third case is the control that keeps the gate from being satisfied by never making the claim at all.

`nextStrategyChoice` in `landView.test.ts` is the branches page's "How to land them" picker, pulled out of the page because there is no harness here for a page's state. The inventory is re-read after every row action and whenever an earlier batch finishes, while the selection is kept, and each re-read wrote the server's default over the picker — so a batch picked as squashes was queued as merges into the operator's own checkout, under a confirmation that did not repeat the strategy (it does now). What is pinned is that a read changes only the default, that a pick survives it, and that clearing or queueing the selection hands the next one back to the default. `RunLand` already held the two apart and is not under this test.
