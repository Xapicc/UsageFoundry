# Styling, tokens, colour and variants

[← conventions index](../conventions.md)

Read before editing `src/app/globals.css` (its layers, tokens, palette, focus ring and motion) or a component's variant props.

  - **The palette and the type scale are AppKit's, not the web's.** Surfaces are four steps rather than three: `--bg` is the window, `--bg-raised` a card or a sheet, `--bg-inset` a *recess* into one (a field, a meter track, a log), and `--bg-grouped` a grouped list inside a card. The last one moves in opposite directions per scheme — AppKit lightens a grouped row against a dark window and darkens it against a light one — so one hex pair cannot express it and `light-dark()` is what carries the difference; it is also why a bezeled control is `--bezel` and never `--bg-inset`, which is the recess a text field sits in and reads as the same object at a glance. `--border` is a hairline at AppKit weight, far lighter than the box border it replaced, and `--shadow-e1` is now barely more than a seam, so a `default` card reads as a grouped box separated by its hairline rather than a card floating off the page. Body is 13px on the SF stack (`-apple-system` first, so macOS resolves SF rather than whatever `system-ui` has been remapped to), and `--text-sm` is 13px as well, deliberately: AppKit sets a control's own text at body size, and the two names stay apart so a call site still says which of the two it meant and the body step can move again without dragging every control with it. Nothing shouts — `CardTitle` and `Th` are sentence case at a weight step, never uppercase with tracking, and the legacy `th` rule matches them.
  - **There is one accent, and two different things are measured against it.** `--accent` is the *text* blue and clears 4.5:1 as text on `--bg` and `--bg-raised` in both schemes. `--tint`/`--tint-fg` is the fill-and-label pair a filled control wears, and in dark it is a different, deeper blue, because no single one does both jobs — `#4a9bff` carries white at 2.8:1. Where the browser exposes the operator's own accent, `--tint`/`--tint-fg` and everything mixed from `--accent-source` (the ring, the selection wash) switch to it; it is taken as a **pair**, because `AccentColorText` is specified as the colour of text placed on `AccentColor` and that is the only thing keeping a Graphite or Yellow accent legible under its own label. `--accent` is deliberately left alone — nothing guarantees an arbitrary accent reads as body text on a card. The `@supports` test names the whole `color-mix(in oklab, AccentColor …)` expression rather than the keyword alone: the two features shipped years apart, and a browser with one and not the other would leave `--ring` invalid at computed-value time, which is a focus ring in `currentColor` — visible, wrong and silent. `Highlight`/`HighlightText` was the other candidate and is rejected: engines return the selection *wash* there rather than the accent, and a white label on a pale wash is the failure this split exists to prevent.
  - **`--danger` is split the same way, and for the same arithmetic.** `--danger` is the *mark*: a status tone, a chart mark, the `text-danger` on a badge or a deleted diff line, and a `bg-danger` fill with no label on it. `--danger-solid`/`--danger-fg` is the fill-and-label pair, and the danger button is the only call site that wears it. On a dark card the two jobs do not overlap — `text-danger` needs the colour over Y 0.280 to clear 4.5:1 and white-on-fill needs it under Y 0.183 — so the dark `--danger` that reads as a mark carried white at 2.82:1 until the pair existed. `--ring-danger` and `--danger-line` stay on `--danger` because a focus halo and a hairline are drawn on the page rather than under a label, which is the same line `--accent-source` draws. The trade the split makes is the one `--tint` makes: the deeper fill stands off a dark page less well, and the label wins, because the label is what identifies a filled control.
  - **One focus treatment, stated in one place.** `@layer base` draws `outline: 2px solid var(--ring)` at 2px offset on everything focusable, and the legacy sheet no longer sets `outline: none` on `input:focus` — that suppression is what made a *second* treatment necessary, and it is why a button and the input beside it used to wear different rings. `--shadow-focus` is gone with it; a second way of saying this is how the two drifted apart. A component may override the ring's **colour** and nothing else, which is how `Button` keeps `--ring-danger` on the one control that cannot be undone, and why no component in the kit states an outline width or offset.
  - **Tokens.** Colour and type as above, plus: `--motion-fast|base|slow` (120/180/240ms — a control answering the pointer, something arriving or leaving, something travelling a distance) and `--ease-standard`/`--ease-entrance`; `--control-h` (32px, the floor for anything clickable) and `--control-h-lg` (36px, a button or a text input), stated as `min-h-[var(…)]` so a control keeps its box when its content changes; `--ring`/`--ring-danger` (the focus halo, at 45%) and `--shadow-press` (inset, so a pressed control recesses without moving); `--selection`, the accent wash behind selected text, at an alpha low enough that `--fg` keeps its own contrast whatever accent it turns out to be; `--ok-line`/`--warn-line`/`--danger-line`/`--accent-line`, each tone at 40%, for an *edge* that has to say which tone without outshouting the text it annotates — full strength stays for a conditional warning, the tint is for anything permanently on screen; `--fill-hover`/`--fill-active`, a neutral wash at 8% and 14% of `--fg`, translucent rather than a hex pair because it lands on three different surfaces and has to darken a light one and lighten a dark one — every opaque token in the palette is within 2% of `--bg-inset` in light mode and reads as no change at all. The shell adds three of its own: `--sidebar-w` (14rem, swapped for `--sidebar-w-rail` at 3.5rem by `[data-sidebar="collapsed"]`) and `--toolbar-h` (52px, a floor rather than a height — see the shell notes below). `--canvas-dot` is the workflow canvas's ground, and it is a token rather than the `color-mix` written into the utility that reads it: Lightning CSS emits a fallback declaration for a `color-mix` it cannot resolve, and that fallback is the *first operand at full strength* — a grid of solid `--fg` dots on any browser without it. Behind a `var()` it gets the `@supports` pair every other translucent token here gets. Nine named utilities (`grep -c '^@utility' src/app/globals.css`): `hatched`, `mono`, `ui-transition`, `skeleton`, `sheet-enter`, `drawer-enter`, `dot-grid`, and `app-drag`/`app-no-drag` for Window Controls Overlay — named rather than written as arbitrary values because the property is `-webkit-app-region`, which has no unprefixed spelling that ships anywhere.
  - **Motion is explanation.** `ui-transition` is the one transition an interactive surface wears; it deliberately omits `transform` and `width`, because a control must not move or resize between its states. Nothing loops except the in-flight spinner and `skeleton`, both of which stay legible frozen — `@layer base` flattens every duration under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and that blanket is not to be defeated with a `!important` or a JS-driven animation. The spinner is two marks and three call sites: `Spinner`'s CSS ring and `Button`'s busy ring under the plain skin, and `.uf-spin` under the ascii one, which `Spinner`, `Button` **and** `StatusMark`'s `running` all draw — so under that skin a *leading status mark* loops, which is the one looping element that appears several times in one column. It freezes on a three-quarter block, which reads "not finished" for the reason the ring is drawn broken rather than whole, and `globals.css` carries the two measurements it rests on: which glyphs in the block range share one advance width, and why the box is `1ch` with the glyph at half an em inside it.

  - **Numbers behave.** Every figure `tabular-nums` (`Stat`, `Th`/`Td` with `num`, `Meter`, `mono`) so it cannot jitter as the page polls, units attached, and an unknown reading says so — the hatched indeterminate meter, never a 0% bar. Reserve space for anything that streams in.

- **Styling** is Tailwind v4, CSS-first — no `tailwind.config.js`, no CSS modules, no third-party component library. Primitives live in `src/components/ui/`. Four things in `src/app/globals.css` are load-bearing and were each arrived at by measurement, not preference:
  - **The `@layer theme, base, legacy, components, utilities` statement.** Layer precedence is declared, never inferred: `next dev` and `next build` emit the blocks in *different* orders, so an inferred order means the legacy sheet beats utilities in the browser and loses to them in the shipped image.
  - **`@import "tailwindcss" source(none)` plus an explicit `@source`.** Automatic content detection respects `.gitignore`, and `.dockerignore` hides `.gitignore` from the build context — so the container would scan `CLAUDE.md` and `tsconfig.tsbuildinfo` and emit a different stylesheet than the host. A class would work in `next dev` and be missing from the image.
  - **Tokens keep their existing semantic names** (`--bg-raised`, `--fg-muted`, `--warn`) and `@theme inline` maps them to utilities. `inline` is mandatory — without it a utility freezes one theme's value at the definition site. It also decides whether a token can be overridden at all, and in the opposite direction from how it reads: a theme entry whose value is a `var()` emits that `var()` into the utility and resolves per element, while a **literal is baked in**. `--radius-sm: 6px` made `rounded-sm` emit `border-radius: 6px` at 127 call sites, which no later selector could reach, which is why radius, elevation, the two font stacks and the body line-height now sit in `:root` as `--corner-*`, `--elevation-*`, `--family-*` and `--leading-ui` with the `@theme` entry pointing at them. Check the emitted stylesheet before believing an override lands. Two of Tailwind's own radius utilities are static and bake in a literal no token can reach — `rounded-full`, which is `calc(infinity * 1px)`, and bare `rounded`, which is `.25rem`: a box with its ends taken off takes `rounded-pill` (`--corner-pill`, 9999px and 0 under the ascii skin), and `rounded-full` is reserved for a mark that must be a **circle** in both skins, because a flattened status dot is a square and the knowledge graph and the conflicts map both read a square as a different kind of node. Three theme states come from `color-scheme` + `light-dark()`: absent `[data-theme]` follows the OS, the two explicit values override it. Do not put `font-size` back on `html`; it redefines `rem` and silently shrinks every spacing and type utility by 12.5%.
  - **Variants are typed props with `Record<Union, string>` lookup maps, never `data-[…]` Tailwind variants.** Three reasons, all silent: React renders `data-x={false}` as the string `"false"`, which a bare `data-x:` variant matches on presence; Tailwind scans source as text, so an interpolated `text-${tone}` emits nothing; and `Meter` carries both `data-sev="ok"` and `data-unknown="true"` for an unknown reading (`severityFor(null)` is `"ok"`), where losing the tiebreak paints a solid green 100% bar instead of the hatched indeterminate one. `src/components/Meter.test.tsx` pins that last one. Shared tone unions live in `src/lib/format.ts`, not beside the components, because `src/lib` is what `tsconfig.test.json` compiles. The three attribute selectors on `:root` are not exceptions to this and must not be read as a precedent for one: `[data-theme]`, `[data-skin]` and `[data-sidebar]` are *document* states written before React exists, by the pre-paint script, precisely because they have to be settled before the first paint. They change `color-scheme`, geometry and token values, and no component reads any of them as a variant. `[data-skin="ascii"]` is what took that past geometry and `color-scheme`, and it is the line to hold rather than the one to cite: it restates values the default skin already states, renames no token, and a component that branched on the skin — in a `Record` map or a `data-skin:` variant — is exactly what this rule forbids. It is a second axis rather than a fourth `[data-theme]` value so that all six combinations stay reachable and switching the skin on cannot discard the OS setting.
  - The `@layer legacy { … }` block is the migration's remaining surface and shrinks as pages move onto the kit. It still beats Preflight and still loses to utilities. When it is empty, delete it — and with it `--radius`, `--mono` and `--sans`, which exist only for its sake.
