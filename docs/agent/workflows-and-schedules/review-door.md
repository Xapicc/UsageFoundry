# The review door: how a review block starts its reviews against the assist queue and the instance limit

[← workflows-and-schedules index](../workflows-and-schedules.md)

Read before editing `atReviewDoor` or the passage `driveReviewItem` makes through it in `src/lib/workflows.ts`, the `busy`, `held` and `alreadyRunning` refusals of `startReview` in `src/lib/review.ts`, or `enforceInstanceBudgetForBlock` where a review block calls it. The rest of a review block is in [review-blocks-and-providers.md](review-blocks-and-providers.md).

**A full assist queue is waited out at both doors a review passes, and one instance's branches pass the first one at a time.** `atReviewDoor` queues them from `driveReviewItem`'s `assistBudgetFull()` check to `startReview`'s return, by which `startAssist` has written the row that fills the slot. Without it, siblings asking in the same tick each passed one free slot and the instance guard at the same banked figure: two to four billed reviews at one slot, measured 2026-10-17, and money past the workflow's limit. Serialising was first turned down because it cannot replace the wait on its own; it was taken on 2026-10-25 beside the wait, because the overshoot is a guard failure, at the cost of an instance preparing its diffs one after another. The queue moves on however a passage ends, a throw included. It is keyed on the instance and not the block, because the limit is the instance's: it was per block when it landed, and two review blocks side by side in one graph, each with a queue of its own, passed one free slot and the guard at $0 in the same tick (found 2026-10-25, and `reviewBlockRun.test.ts`' two-block case holds both reads at one await and fails with two reviews and $0.04 against a $0.01 limit when the key is the block). The wider key cannot deadlock because nothing inside a passage waits on another review passing a door and a passage never re-enters the queue; a wait added there on another block's review would hang the instance. It stops short of exact in two places. The guard figure holds reviews that have *ended*, so past one slot reviews running together are not in it and the overshoot is bounded by `maxConcurrentAssists`; and a fix run's cycle-boundary guard reads the same figure without passing the door, so a review start and a fix run's boundary can still read it at the same banked value in one tick, which nothing covers. A chat turn or another block can still take a slot while `runDiff` reads the diff, so `startReview`'s own door finds the queue full. Until 2026-10-06 that refusal was written `set-aside` as "it could not be reviewed", and four approvable branches at the default cap of two came out two approved, two set aside, the merge behind landing half the work over a queue. `startReview` now marks a refusal by the process budget `busy`, decided by `assistDoor` at the moment it refused rather than inferred by asking `assistBudgetFull()` again afterwards, which reads a later moment: a slot taken during the window scan would turn a spent window into a wait. Only the budget is `busy`: a spent window or install limit is an answer no wait would change and still sets the branch aside, and the shutdown refusal outranks the budget and is never `busy`. A halt or a shutdown ends either wait at the top of `driveReviewItem`'s loop, because `reviewStillOpen` answers false to both, leaving the block for the halt or `reconcileBlocksOnBoot` to settle. Another review of the same run is waited out too, the Review button's or another block's, which `startReview` refuses `alreadyRunning`: set aside over it, the branch was judged by a collision, and the block's own review asks a verdict that one may not. The install-wide hold is waited out at both doors the same way (`startReview` answers `held` to `refuseWhileHeld`) and holds the block at its claim before that; `run-lifecycle/fleet-stop-hold-and-set-aside.md` says why.
