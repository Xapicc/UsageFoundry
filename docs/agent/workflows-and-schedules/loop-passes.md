# Loop passes: scheduling, run counts, members and `looping`

[← workflows-and-schedules index](../workflows-and-schedules.md)

Read before editing `stepPass` or `loopSpend` in `src/lib/workflows.ts`, `passMemberId`/`passNumberOf` in `src/lib/passIds.ts`, `passesOf` in `src/lib/format.ts`, `worstCaseRuns` or `worstCaseMembers` in `src/lib/canvasGraph.ts`, `MAX_FAN_OUT` or `MAX_LOOP_RUNS` in `src/lib/apiTypes.ts`, the instance page's passes and `loopSpendShown` in `src/app/workflows/[id]/instances/[instanceId]/page.tsx`, or the `looping` block status.

**A pass is the section instantiated, and there is one scheduler.** A pass runs through `planInstanceStep` over the **section's own** nodes and edges, with member state keyed per pass — the same function that releases run blocks, spawns orchestrator turns, waits for every run an orchestrator emitted, and queues a merge block with the runs its incoming edges resolved to. `stepPass` is the writing half and holds no reading of its own: a second answer to "which member may go now" is a second place for a member to be left waiting for ever, and a workflow that is simply never finished looks exactly like one that is still working. `passState` is the whole adapter — a pass's member rows in the terms that function already reads — and `passNode` is the other half, handing `upsertBlock`, `claimBlock`, `planNode`, `startBlockTurn` and `startMergeBlock` the member's own row id on the node itself rather than threading a second id through five signatures, where passing the graph's where the row's was meant would look up nothing and silently write nothing. `blockNode` is the way back: a ledger row's id names the pass, so every block that spawns something resolves its node through one resolver, and a second one that forgot the pass spelling would answer `undefined` and settle a live member as no longer part of its workflow.

**`MAX_LOOP_RUNS` is the arithmetic nobody does, and an orchestrator member's fan-out is spent per pass.** A fan-out cap is what an orchestrator block may start *each time it is reached*, and a section reaches it once a pass — so a pass is one run per run member, **plus** for each orchestrator member the deciding turn and every run its cap allows. That is the whole cost of letting one into a section, and it is why the worst case is no longer passes × members. It is refused at Save with **every factor named** — passes, the members, the fan-out cap that is spent again on each pass, and a review member's fix rounds — because a cap on the product alone is a number the operator cannot act on. `worstCaseRuns` in `canvasGraph.ts` is that arithmetic written once — the refusal calls it, and it is the figure both the editor's statement and `/workflows/[id]`'s loop row print, because two copies once disagreed by exactly the deciding turn and the editor stated "up to 60 runs" over a graph Save refused at 72. A member's `fixRounds` is **required** there, nullable, and the editor reads its drafts through `worstCaseMembers`, because an editor call site that passed kind and fan-out alone still typechecked while the field was optional, and stated "up to 20 runs" over a section with a review member that Save refused at 80. It answers **null** rather than guessing where a pass cap or a fan-out is blank, since both are refused at Save and a blank read as zero approves an unbounded press of Run on the operator's behalf. The limit stays 60 and was **not** raised for the fan-out term: three run blocks and the merge that lands them, at the full `MAX_LOOP_PASSES`, is exactly 60, so the ordinary section still clears it and what the term costs is an orchestrator's room — which is the spend the operator has least visibility of. **A retried deciding turn is not in the figure.** An orchestrator member's `turnRetries` starts its deciding turn again when that turn failed, and a retry is spend but not a run: it creates no `workflow_instance_runs` row, so `worstCaseRuns` and `MAX_LOOP_RUNS` do not count it, and the statement the editor prints is a count of runs and says so. What bounds it is what bounds a deciding turn anyway — `chatTurnBudgetUSD` per attempt, at most `turnRetries` more attempts per member per pass — and the money is seen where money is seen: `loopSpend` sums the block's cost columns, so `maxLoopCostUSD` and the instance guard both count every attempt. It is deliberately larger than `MAX_WORKFLOW_NODES`, since these runs arrive one pass at a time over hours rather than in one creating turn: what it bounds is the bill, not what a single event-loop turn claims.

**`MAX_FAN_OUT` stays 10 though the taskboard sweep fills it, because a wider batch lands worse and the pass cap is not what runs out.** Measured read-only against this install's database on 2026-10-07 at `d85106c0`: 27 of 63 deciding turns emitted exactly 10 runs, and three of four workflows set fan-out 10 with 5 passes — the most `MAX_LOOP_RUNS` admits at 10, since a pass is the turn plus its runs and 5 × 11 is 55 where 6 would be 66 — yet only 2 of 26 settled loops reached their pass cap. So a full fan-out costs a sweep more passes, which it has room for, not tasks it cannot reach. Raising the fan-out alone buys nothing here, because the passes shrink to fit: for a section that is one orchestrator, at 15 a sweep gets 3 passes and 45 emitted runs where it now gets 5 and 50, and no fan-out up to 20 reaches more than 57 — so it is a gain only with `MAX_LOOP_RUNS` raised as well, two widenings of what one press may start unattended. And the git history says batches already pay for their width at landing (`docs/verification/isolation-and-landing.md`, 2026-10-08): 18 batches of 10 landed 179 of 180 runs, but 23% of those landings first needed a conflicting back-merge — a billed `resolveConflicts` turn each — against 17% in batches of 1–5, and the rate climbs with the landings the target took while a run worked, 0 of 128 at none to 45% at seven to ten, which is where every sibling past the tenth would land. What would lower the rate is not either ceiling: 45 of the 76 conflicted landings conflicted in `docs/verification.md`, whose count columns every new *Verified* entry edited; they were removed on 2026-10-08 for that reason, so the figure is the rate before the removal. Re-measure with `python3 scripts/batch-landing-conflicts.py` before revisiting this; a batch is inferred from base commit and start time, and nothing above 11 exists to measure.

**Nothing is manufactured within a pass, and nothing at all is carried between passes.** Members carry each other's branches exactly as the section's own links say — `edgeVerdict` resolves them and `createRun` records them — and the pass's merge block lands what its predecessors left, which is what `edgeVerdict` already resolves for a merge block. Across passes there is no `continue_branch` and no ref of any kind: pass N+1's runs cut **fresh** branches in their folders, and what makes pass N visible to them is that it **landed**. That is simpler than the chain it replaced and it is also the only thing that can be true of a section that forks, where there is no last-run-of-a-chain to hand on. `stepPass` creates within one event-loop turn for `startWorkflow`'s reason — `createRun`'s folder claim is check-then-act and atomic only inside one turn, so every `planNode` is done before the first creation — but a member that cannot be created is now **blocked rather than rolled back**: a pass is a graph released a member at a time, so a member that cannot start is a settled predecessor and `planInstanceStep`'s own cascade writes off what was behind it in the same call. The loop then stops because the pass did not complete, which is where that decision belongs. **A member is created once.** `stepPass` skips a creation whose member row already exists, whatever made the pass's state miss it, and a run whose membership could not be recorded is stopped and **not** reported as a write: a run outside `workflow_instance_runs` is one `stopInstance`, the instance guard, `loopSpend` and the caps cannot see, and a step reported as progress is what had `advanceLoop` create the same member again, step after step, until `createRun` ran out of worktree slots at 64. `openPass` writes every member down `waiting` **before** any of them is released, and that is what makes "has this pass finished" a fact on the rows rather than a count somebody keeps: a pass whose entry run exists and whose merge block does not yet would otherwise read as settled, and the next pass would start on top of it.

**The instance page draws a pass as the group of members it is, and `passesOf` is the one reader that decides what is in one.** A pass is several blocks of several kinds now, so `groupPasses` is gone with the null case it existed for: one run per pass was what a loop repeating its own task looked like, and that mode ended when a loop became a frame. What replaces it takes rows by their **member id** rather than by `emittedBy` — `passMember` on the wire, read on the server by `passMemberOf`, because the id's format is `passMemberId`'s and a second parser of it in the browser is how a three-pass loop over a two-block section comes to be drawn as six passes. `emittedBy` may only fold a row under a **member** of the same pass, never under any value it holds: every member run of a pass also names its loop there — that is what released it — so a map over every value folds each of them under the loop and drops it out of its own pass, which is an agent that spent money with nothing on screen saying it ran. A run an orchestrator member decided on is drawn under that member and counted in the pass's own figure, because a fan-out is exactly the part of a press of Run nobody approved one by one. Members are ordered by the loop's `bodyNodeIds` off the **instance's** copy of the graph, so a pass keeps the order it ran in however the workflow has been rewired since: position is two separate sequences here, one for runs and one for ledger rows, so interleaving by it draws a merge member ahead of the runs it landed, and a member that never started has no `startedAt` at all. A member outside that list sorts **last** rather than being dropped — an instance written before the field existed reads every member that way, and the ordering may not be the thing that hides a billed run. The pass's **landing** is on its own heading rather than on the merge member it is read off, because it is the fact that decides whether the next pass could see this one's work; it is stated only once that member has run, since "landed 0 of 0" on a pass still working is a claim that the next pass starts from nothing. A pass's members are drawn here and **not** in the ledger table above, or one block is on the page twice with two accounts of it. The block's own row stays in **passes** — `COUNT(DISTINCT pass)`, unchanged — and this card is where those passes are opened up.

**`passMemberId` owns the spelling of a member id and `passNumberOf` is the only thing that reads one back.** `loopPasses` groups rows into passes on the pass number written into the id rather than on a count, and the two failures are opposite and both silent: three passes of two read as six passes of one trips the cap four passes early and records it as the loop running out, while six read as one never trips it at all. The same number is why the block's count column is `COUNT(DISTINCT pass)` and not `COUNT(*)` — it stays a count of **passes** wherever it is rendered, which is the unit the cap was set in. The id is read at its **first** `#pass-N`, and `passNumberOf` is `passMemberOf`'s pass rather than a second pattern: `pass-2` is a legal block id and a legal spec id, so an id can carry the spelling twice, and two readers that took different ones filed a pass-1 member called `pass-2` under a loop called `L#pass-1` — `passState` never saw it, and every step created it again. The first is always the loop's, because a node id cannot contain `#` and a loop cannot sit in another loop's section. **And the string alone cannot say there is a loop at all**: outside every loop `createEmitted` names a run `<block>#<spec>`, so a top-level orchestrator `o` deciding on spec `pass-2` writes `o#pass-2` — the body-less spelling of pass 2 of a loop `o`. Every reader that holds the instance therefore reads through `passMemberIn`, which takes the parse only when the instance's graph says `o` is a loop; read as a pass, such a run was offered no pick-up and leaving it behind was refused for a loop that is not there. Refusing `pass-N` as a spec id was the alternative and was not taken: a model may reasonably write one, and block ids would then accept a spelling spec ids refuse. The readers left on bare `passMemberOf` are the ones only ever handed ids under a real loop — `loopPasses`, `groupPasses`, and the pass count `blocksOf` reads for a loop's row alone — and `land.ts`, whose hold joins on a `looping` block and whose batch owner reads a merge block's id, which no orchestrator writes. A **body-less** pass keeps the id it has always had, `…#pass-N` with no third part, because there is no member to name and every row already in a database says that; `bootBlocks.test.ts` builds one by hand. **Every row a loop causes is named under `passPrefix`**, not only its members: `createEmitted` names a run an orchestrator member decided on `<memberId>#<specId>`, so it carries the prefix too, and `loopSpend` sums on that prefix rather than on `emitted_by` — a pass whose spend is understated is a spend cap that never trips, and the money a loop spends on a section that fans out is mostly in the runs a model decided on rather than in its own deciding turn. A member whose `runs` row has gone is an **empty slot rather than a missing pass**, since the member row still says which pass it belongs to, and a pass all of whose runs have gone survives as a pass with no runs — which is `planLoopPass`'s "started nothing" stop, reachable from real data rather than only from a creation that threw. A member id carrying no pass number at all becomes a pass of its own, the safe direction: an extra entry can only stop a loop early, where folding two passes into one lets it run past the cap.

**`looping` is the one exception to "a block adds no status", and it is an exception rather than a precedent.** `BlockStatus` is a lifecycle and a merge block reuses `thinking`/`emitted` verbatim precisely so that liveness, halts and boot reconciliation keep reading two words — the argument on `BlockStatus` itself, and it is the right one. A loop cannot: between passes it has no child in flight and is still not settled, so `thinking` would be a lie for the gap and `emitted` would release the block behind it while a whole further pass could still be committed to the folders behind it. So it counts as live everywhere `thinking` does, and **every** site that docblock warns about names it: `memberTally`'s live count, `haltBlocks`' sweep, `reconcileBlocksOnBoot`, `loopVerdict`, `liveBlocksOf` — which is the second press of Run, and reads the list off `LIVE_BLOCK_STATUSES` rather than a second copy of it — and `land.ts`'s `loopStillRepeating`. A halt writes it `failed` rather than `blocked` — the passes it took were billed — and a restart closes it out for the queued-run reason, since another pass would be an unattended agent started hours after anyone asked for it; **unless a member of its instance survived that same boot**, which is the per-instance question `bootBlockPlan` already answers for the `waiting` blocks beside it, because the pass such a loop is on is the run `reconcileOnBoot` deliberately kept — nothing under it was closed out, and the pass the next advance starts is the one already being paid for. Its own `cost_usd` stays 0: a loop spawns nothing, and every pass's spend is on the rows that pass caused — its members, the ledger rows of its orchestrator and merge members, and the runs an orchestrator member decided on, all of which `loopSpend` reaches through one id prefix.

**A pass asked to be the last is stepped exactly like any other, and no pass is opened after it.** "Stop after this pass" (`loop-termination.md`) changes nothing `stepPass` does: `advanceLoop` still steps an unsettled pass before it reads anything the decision needs, so the pass in flight runs its members, its orchestrator turns and its merge as it would have, and the request is read only through `planLoopPass` once that pass has settled. What it prevents is `openPass`, which is reached only on a `pass` decision — so no member row of a further pass is ever written, and `loopPasses` counts the passes the loop actually took. The control lives on the loop card and is offered while the block is `looping` with at least one pass; a halt writes the block `failed`, so Stop pressed as well removes the control from the page instead of leaving a button the server would refuse, and a press that races the pass settling earns a 409 and a re-read rather than a wait for the next poll.

**The card's cost figure is the shown reading with the pass blocks' turns added, and it says what the limit is checked against instead of showing it.** `loopSpendShown` sums the member runs' shown `spentUSD` and the reported `costUSD` of the pass's ledger members — an orchestrator's deciding turn and a merge's conflict resolution — because `loopSpend` counts those, and a card of runs alone could sit well under a limit the loop had just stopped on. A turn nothing measured is left out rather than counted as `$0.00`, and the "N of M reported a cost" clause stays a count of runs, since it is read beside the run count. The figure still excludes what the guard adds — estimates for cycles killed before they reported — and that figure is never rendered (`AggregateDTO.costGuardUSD`), so under the limit the card carries one clause: *Checked against a figure that also counts cycles cut short before they reported*, only when a limit is set. The label reads "Spent by its passes" because the sum is no longer runs alone.
