# Replacing and deciding proposals

[← chat index](../chat.md)

Read before editing `createProposalReplacing` or `rejectProposal` in `src/lib/chat.ts`, or how `src/app/chat/page.tsx` draws pending, decided and superseded proposals.

**A proposal still waiting can be replaced by a corrected one in a single tool call, and a decided one can never be.** `propose_run` and `propose_workflow` take `supersedes`, naming a proposal of the same chat by the label the model gave it or by the id that came back; `createProposalReplacing` marks that row `superseded`, records `superseded_by`, and writes the replacement — both statements in one transaction, and the first is a conditional `UPDATE … WHERE id=? AND chat_id=? AND status='pending'`, which is `rejectProposal`'s shape and is there for `rejectProposal`'s reason. What it replaces is two clicks and a billed turn: reject the card, say why, propose again. **What the conditional buys is the case where the operator pressed Approve while the model was writing the correction** — the UPDATE matches no row, and the whole call fails with nothing written. A replacement created beside an approved run is a second agent in one folder for one job, authorised once, and that is worse than a refused tool call by enough that the trade is not close. `superseded` is a fifth `ProposalStatus` rather than a deletion, for `chat_questions`' reason — the thread has to read as what happened, and a card that vanished reads as one nobody was ever shown — and it is **decided**, so every door that offers a proposal for decision already refuses it: the route scopes its ids to `pendingProposals`, and `planProposal`'s first refusal covers anything that reaches `approveProposal` another way. It is not a failure and is not drawn as one. **The label survives the correction**, and that is the half a caller could get wrong in silence: a replacement naming no label of its own inherits the one it replaced, decided inside the transaction by the function that knows which row it replaced rather than by the tool door, because a dropped label is a sibling started immediately instead of after the run it was told to wait for — bit-for-bit a sibling that was never told. The uniqueness check on a label the replacement names for *itself* stops counting the card being replaced, since uniqueness is among undecided proposals and that one is about to be decided — and the rival check on a carried-on branch stops counting it for the same reason, or no card that sets `continueBranch` could be corrected at all; and both places that resolve a label to a row — `approveRunBatch`'s outside map and `propose_run`'s own — refuse to let a replaced card displace the replacement that inherited its label, because the two can share a `created_at` and the tiebreak below it is a uuid. A supersede counts against nothing: it adds no card to the panel, so `MAX_PENDING_PROPOSALS` may not see it. Cross-kind is allowed in both directions, since what is being recorded is only that this card replaced that one. **A workflow or schedule card replacing a run card that a waiting sibling's `dependsOn` names is told, not refused**: `strandedDependents` appends a sentence to the tool's reply naming each such sibling by title and id and saying the click will refuse it. The supersede turns a chain that could be wired into one that cannot, but it is the correction `supersedes` exists for, and a refusal would only make the model re-point every sibling before it may make it — while a sibling left pointing there is refused by name at the click rather than started unchained, which is the direction that fails safe. What the reply removes is the model learning nothing at the one call where it could act. And `list_proposals` reports the link both ways, because a `superseded` row on its own reads as work that never happened and is then proposed a third time.

**A replaced proposal is drawn in the decided list and nowhere else, and every count on the panel excludes it by construction rather than by remembering to.** `src/app/chat/page.tsx` splits the thread's proposals once — `pending` is `status === "pending"` and `decided` is everything else — and the waiting badge, the sidebar's `pendingCount`, `Select all`, the counted sentence above the approve row and the ids a click sends are every one of them derived from `pending`. So `superseded` cost the panel no filters at all, which is the property to keep: a fifth status subtracted in five places is a status one of the five forgets, and the one that would forget it is `Select all`, which would then tick what the route drops — the exact contradiction `approvable` exists to have none of. What the card gains is a line, for the reason a settled question keeps its place one column over: it says **Replaced by** and names the card that took it, because a decided row reading `superseded` with nothing beside it is work that never happened. It names the **title** rather than the label, which is the one handle that looks right and is not — `createProposalReplacing` hands the `spec_id` over on purpose, so both cards carry it and it cannot tell them apart. The link goes one way only, from the replaced card to its replacement, and it is a tab switch and a scroll rather than a cross-referencing mechanism: the two are almost never in the same list, since a replacement is still waiting and what it replaced is decided, and `dependsOn` — the only other place a card names a sibling — deliberately names one without linking it. A replacement swept out from under the row still reads as replaced, because the sentence is written from the row's own `superseded_by` and falls back to naming no card rather than to saying nothing.

**A refused decision is drawn after the row it refused has gone.** The approve row renders only while something is pending and the refusal sentence used to render inside it, so approving the last pending card and being refused — decided in another tab, or replaced by the chat while the list was on screen — emptied `pending` on the next answer and unmounted the message along with the row: the operator pressed Approve, watched the card disappear and was told nothing. The sentence is gated on itself now and the controls above it on the list. `decide` also re-reads the thread on a refusal rather than only on success, because the route answers a refusal with the error alone — every one of its 400s is about the page's picture of what is pending being stale — and the card the sentence is about otherwise goes on reading as pending for up to a poll, which is the page contradicting the sentence under its own button and a second press earning the same refusal. The clearing rule follows: a refusal names a proposal of the thread it was made in, so leaving that thread clears it, and `New chat` has to clear it too now that the sentence can outlive an empty list.
